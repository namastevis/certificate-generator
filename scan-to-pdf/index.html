Why this one matters

Scanner apps are the most invasive software on most people's phones. They ask for the camera, the photo library and the network, and the documents people put through them are exactly the ones that should not travel: bank statements, ID cards, medical letters, property papers.

This does the same job with a web page. The camera feed is handled by your browser, the photographs stay in the tab, and the PDF is assembled on your device. Close the tab and every trace is gone.

Getting a readable scan

  • Flat surface, and light coming from the side rather than over your shoulder — that is what casts the shadow.
  • Fill the frame with the page. Cropping later throws away detail you could have captured.
  • Hold the phone parallel to the paper. A tilted photo makes text at the far edge blurry.
  • Check each thumbnail before building. Retaking now is quicker than discovering it later.

Common questions

Are the photos uploaded?

No. There is no server behind this page. Nothing is written to your camera roll either, unless you download the finished PDF.

The camera will not start.

Browsers only allow camera access on a secure (https) connection and only after you grant permission. If you dismissed the prompt, reload and allow it. On iPhone, Safari handles this more reliably than other browsers.

Is there automatic edge detection and perspective correction?

Not yet — this is the honest gap against a dedicated scanner app. It is on the roadmap and is a genuinely interesting thing to build if you fancy it.

The PDF is huge.

Phone cameras produce enormous photos. Run the result through Compress PDF.
